About these indices
SPI
Standardized Precipitation Index. The year’s total is fitted to a gamma distribution for that cell (baseline years only) and converted to a standard normal score. 0 is normal; ≤ −1 moderate drought or worse; ≤ −1.5 severe; ≤ −2 extreme. Because the input is annual totals this is a 12-month SPI for the calendar year.
Percent of normal
Annual total as a percentage of the baseline mean. Simple, but hard to compare between wet and dry climates.
Z-score
(Total − baseline mean) / baseline standard deviation. Like SPI without the skew correction.
Rainfall Anomaly Index
van Rooy (1965). Departure from the mean scaled by the mean of the 10% wettest (or driest) baseline years; roughly −3 to +3 for the extremes.
Decile
1 = driest 10% of baseline years, 10 = wettest. Based on the fitted distribution.
Departure (mm)
Total minus baseline mean, in millimetres.
Consecutive dry years
Run of years with SPI < 0, counting up to the selected year.
Summaries
Computed over every year in the record: drought frequency (% of years with SPI ≤ −1), severity (sum of −SPI in those years), worst SPI and the year it happened, longest dry spell, coefficient of variation of annual rainfall.

This is meteorological drought from precipitation alone. SPEI, PDSI and aridity indices also need evaporative demand (temperature or vapour pressure deficit), which this dataset doesn’t include. Regions are approximate boxes, area-weighted by cos(latitude).

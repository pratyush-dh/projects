# Side project: crew-reconstructed heights (HTCD 2) vs the regional H–D model

Question: for broken-top trees, do the crews' visual reconstructions of the intact height (HTCD 2) agree with an empirical height–diameter model, where do they diverge, and can the model serve as a QC audit?

Run order (from this folder; needs only the SQLite export and the main study's `height_model_results.csv`, no PostgreSQL):
`01_extract.py` (~4 min, live trees HTCD 1/2/3 with ACTUALHT) → `02_prepare.py` (ecodivision, model height HT_hat, earlier record via PREV_TRE_CN) → `03_analyze.py` → `04_robustness.py` → `05_figures.py` (fig1–3) → `06_regional.py` (tables) → `07_regional_figs.py` (fig4–11) → `08_broken_part.py` (fig12–14). Outputs: `out/`, `figures/`.

## Design corrections to the original plan
1. **The "missing-length" comparison is algebraically the total-height comparison.** (HT − ACTUALHT) − (HT_hat − ACTUALHT) = HT − HT_hat (asserted in code). ACTUALHT cancels, so it adds no independent information; it is only usable as a conditioning covariate. Analysis is on ln(HT / HT_hat).
2. **No ground truth ⇒ no "observer bias".** A crew–model difference is a difference; it cannot say who is wrong, and public FIADB has no crew ID. Fix: an independent anchor — the same tree's earlier field-measured, intact height (HTCD 1 at the previous visit), grown forward with the median growth rate of intact measured trees.
3. **Model error must be benchmarked.** The model is DBH-only (RMSE ≈ 10 ft); its scatter on intact measured trees in unseen plots (control, HTCD 1, test plots only, n = 2.34 M) is the reference for HTCD 2 (n = 199,350 with break > 0).
4. **Severity-ratio axes create spurious trends.** ACTUALHT/HT is coupled to the crew's noise, ACTUALHT/HT_hat and any ACTUALHT rank are coupled to the model's error (a tree whose stem is long at a given DBH is taller than the model expects). Shown by simulation with a *perfect* crew (Fig 2). Severity effects are therefore judged only against the control curve, not against zero.
5. **Trees were not chosen at random**: breakage is not independent of height for a given DBH, so a model fitted on intact trees is not an unbiased "counterfactual intact height" for broken trees (selection). Bias here is a combination of selection, model error and crew judgement.

## Results
* HTCD 2 heights are **3.1 % below** the model (ln ratio −0.032, 95 % plot-clustered CI −0.033 to −0.030) relative to the intact control; class differences −0.9 % to −4.7 %. Spread (SD of ln ratio) is 0.22 for both HTCD 2 and control.
* Against the **earlier measured height** (55,970 trees whose HT ≠ previous HT): crew RMSE 0.19 (log units), model 0.26, fresh measurement of intact trees 0.17. Crew is closer than the model for 68 % of trees, in every DBH class and era. Crew bias vs earlier height ≈ 0 (−0.3 %), small positive for large trees.
* **Caveat that limits this**: HT equals the previous recorded HT for **37 %** of HTCD 2 trees (8 % of intact measured trees), rising from 20 % (≤2004) to 53 % (2020+); 44 % of HTCD 2 heights are multiples of 5 ft (23 % control). Crews probably see or copy the earlier value, so agreement with it is not independent evidence of accuracy (the model, by contrast, cannot see it).
* **QC audit fails**: flagging outside the control's 95 % limits flags 5.6 % of HTCD 2 trees (5 % expected by chance); as a detector of crew errors >20 % (vs the anchor) AUC = 0.54, sensitivity 6 %, i.e. no better than chance. A DBH-only model cannot audit individual crew estimates.
* Data-quality: 43,166 HTCD 2 trees (17 %) have ACTUALHT = HT and 7,202 have no ACTUALHT; the model implies a negative missing length (HT_hat < ACTUALHT) for 13–16 % of broken trees.
* Main-study spin-off: 14,403 of 2.36 M HTCD 1 test-plot trees (0.6 %) have ACTUALHT < HT ("measured" but flagged as truncated); they are in the training data of the main study (negligible, but worth one sentence).

## Regional results (steps 06-07)
What was compared: the regional species x ecodivision Curtis-Arney/Wykoff models, fitted only on HTCD 1 trees of the 80 % training plots (fallback species -> genus -> all; 99 % of HTCD 2 predictions came from the species x division tier), predict HT from DBH for HTCD 2 trees; that prediction is compared with the crew's HT. Control = HTCD 1 intact trees on the 20 % unseen plots.
* **Excess bias** (HTCD 2 minus control, % of model height): significantly different from zero in 21 of 37 ecodivisions and 39 of 48 states. Crews are lower than the model almost everywhere, most in the Appalachian/Great Lakes/New England divisions (M211 -5.4, M221 -5.3, 221 -5.1, 211 -4.0 points) and the Pacific Marine division (M242 -3.0, the largest sample, n = 31,173). The only division where crews are significantly HIGHER is 313 (Arizona/New Mexico steppe, +4.6, n = 1,061); Arizona is the only state clearly positive (with Kansas, Illinois, Alabama, Georgia weakly).
* **The model's own bias is regional too** (intact trees): up to +2.7 % in 313, +2.4 % in M341, +1.6 % in M331 (model under-predicts in the interior West), and its error is largest on the Pacific coast (pooled RMSE about 15-16 ft in M242, 263, 242) and smallest in the Southwest (4-6 ft). Interpret crew-vs-model gaps only after subtracting this (panel d of Fig 6).
* **Size**: the excess is small for saplings (-0.5 % at 1-5 in) and largest at 20-30 in in the eastern and Great Lakes divisions (-8 to -12 points; Fig 9).
* **Against the earlier measurement**, crews beat the model in every division where there are 100+ pairs; the RMSE ratio (model/crew) exceeds 1.5 in the Pacific, Rocky Mountain, Southwest and Texas divisions (Fig 8c).
* **Copied heights** (HT equal to the earlier recorded HT) are most common in Arizona (89 %, 568 pairs), Wyoming, New Mexico, Kentucky, Utah and Montana (>65 %, several with small samples) and least in Illinois, Mississippi, West Virginia and Maine (8-14 %) (Fig 7c) - anchor comparisons for the western states are therefore the least independent.
* Crew heights are visibly rounded (bunching at multiples of 10 ft above about 100 ft; Fig 10); intact measured trees are not.

## The broken-off part and stump height (step 08; fig12-14, out/t12-t14, summary_broken.json)
delta_crew = HT - ACTUALHT, delta_model = HT_hat - ACTUALHT, delta_truth = (earlier measured intact height, grown forward) - ACTUALHT (anchor pairs whose HT differs from the earlier HT; n = 55,970, 50,253 with a positive true missing length).
* **Crew vs model missing length**: rank correlation 0.66 (1-9.9 in), 0.64 (10-19.9), 0.56 (20+); the model implies a NEGATIVE missing length for 13-15 % of trees (Fig 12 top).
* **Both vs the earlier measured missing length** (Fig 12 bottom): the crew tracks it (median crew vs true: 5.0 vs 3.2 ft, 10 vs 12, 20 vs 24, 40 vs 46, 59 vs 67 ft): it overstates very short breaks (median 5 ft for a true 3 ft) and understates breaks longer than about 10 ft by roughly 12-19 %. The model does the same and worsens for long breaks (median 50 ft for a true 67 ft, -25 %).
* **Stump height** (Fig 13): the crew-minus-model difference rises steeply with stump height, but so does the same statistic for intact measured trees (grey): mostly mechanical (a tall stump means a tall tree that the DBH-only model under-predicts). The clean test is error against the earlier measured height (Fig 13 bottom): **crew error is flat across stump height** (about -1 ft below 10 in, about 0 at 10-20 in, +3 to +5 ft above 20 in), whereas **model error falls from +3/+7/+20-25 ft for the shortest stumps to -15/-18/-27 ft for the tallest**. Relative to the expected tree height (ACTUALHT/HT_hat, Fig 14): crew error stays within -0.7 to -0.2 ft in every bin; model error goes from +5 ft to -24 ft; the crew's mean missing length is 0.4-0.7 ft below the measured one everywhere (27.1 vs 27.7 ft for short stumps, 4.8 vs 5.4 ft for the tallest).
* Answer to 'do crews over- or under-estimate when they see a tall stump?': not detectably as a function of stump height; the strong pattern is the model's, not the crews'.
* **Selection trap found and fixed**: an earlier run kept only pairs with earlier height > stump + 1 ft. That selects on the earlier measurement's own noise and made crews look as if they under-estimated tall stumps by 1-3 ft. That filter is now used only for the missing-length scatter (fig12 bottom), never for error analyses.
* Remaining caveat: crews may see the earlier recorded height, so non-identical values can still be partly anchored to it; the flat crew line is therefore an upper bound on their independent accuracy.

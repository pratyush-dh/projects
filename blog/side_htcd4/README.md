# Side project: FIA's own modeled heights (HTCD 4) vs a regional model trained on measured trees

Question: HTCD 4 means "estimated with a model" — FIA's own undocumented imputation, used when no height was recorded at all. How does it compare with a height–diameter model fit independently, using only field-measured (HTCD 1) trees? And which one tracks a tree's own growth history better?

Run order (from this folder; needs `../paper/data/targets_current.csv`, no PostgreSQL for step 1; `03_extract_anchor.py` needs the SQLite export, `04_anchor.py` also needs `../side_htcd2/data/trees_htcd123.csv`, extracted for the HTCD 1/2/3 side project — rerun `../side_htcd2/01_extract.py` first if that file is missing):
`01_compare.py` → `02_figs.py` (fig1–5) → `03_extract_anchor.py` → `04_anchor.py` (fig6).

## Method
`../paper/data/targets_current.csv` already has, for every non-measured tree on each state's current-evaluation plots, both the value stored in FIADB (`old_ht`) and the height predicted by the main study's regional species x ecodivision Curtis–Arney/Wykoff model (`new_ht`, fit only on HTCD 1 training-plot trees; see `paper/fit_height_models.py`). Filtering to `old_htcd == 4` gives 190,356 trees. This is a comparison of two **imputation methods** on trees whose true height is unknown to either one — not a validation by itself. Two things anchor it to something real:
1. `../side_htcd2/out/t8_division.csv` — the same regional model's own bias/RMSE on **measured** trees on unseen plots, division by division (what "the model itself is imperfect here anyway" looks like).
2. A **fresh anchor test** (steps 03–04): for HTCD 4 trees whose `PREV_TRE_CN` points to a tree that was itself measured and intact at an earlier inventory, grow that earlier height forward (median ln growth rate by diameter class, from 388,912 HTCD1-to-HTCD1 remeasurement pairs **within CA/OR/WA** — not the national rate) and compare both `old_ht` and `new_ht` against it. n = 143,495 pairs (75% of the 190,356).

## Findings
* **CA, OR and WA hold 99.3%** of all 190,356 current-evaluation HTCD 4 trees (59,693 / 74,222 / 55,050); the other 13 states together hold 1,391 trees (0.7%), scattered singles-to-low-hundreds, and are not mappable or individually reliable on their own (as a pooled group they run substantially negative, -6.8%, unlike the CA/OR/WA population -- but with 13 different states mixed together this is not attributable to any one place). Six ecodivisions (all inside CA/OR/WA) hold 99.1%.
* **Overall, FIA's stored HTCD 4 height is 0.82% above** our regional model (95% CI 0.52–1.11%, ~1.6 ft); within CA/OR/WA specifically, +0.88%. The bias grows with size: near zero for saplings and 10–15 in trees, up to +2.3% (15–20 in) and +3.1% (20–30 in).
* **By division**, most of the difference is explained by the model's own regional bias on measured trees (Marine M242, 89,320 trees: 0.00 pp excess once you subtract the model's own -0.11% bias there). Two smaller-but-still-solid divisions have a real excess: Mediterranean M261 (55,662 trees, +2.08 pp) and M333 Temperate Desert (8,011 trees, +3.71 pp).
* **The tallest trees diverge sharply.** FIA's stored heights reach up to 277 ft; our model tops out at 208 ft (bounded by what the training data show for that DBH). 1,079 HTCD 4 trees are stored above 200 ft; our model only predicts 131 above 200 ft. 952 of the 1,079 are Douglas-fir in the Marine division. The ten most extreme disagreements are all Douglas-fir, 257–277 ft stored vs 166–206 ft predicted.
* **Species**: Douglas-fir, the dominant HTCD 4 species (54,755 trees, 29%), shows almost no bias (+0.2%, 82.6 vs 81.0 ft mean). Western hemlock and white fir run a bit high (+1.8%, +1.9%); redwood +2.1% (90.9 vs 87.3 ft); grand fir -1.5%.
* **The anchor test reverses the story.** Against the growth-projected earlier measured height, FIA's HTCD 4 value is far closer than our DBH-only model: RMSE 4.9 ft vs 16.8 ft; FIA is closer for 84% of trees; R² of FIA's value against the anchor is 0.987 vs 0.840 for our model. The gap widens sharply with tree size (RMSE 3.2 vs 25.2 ft for 30+in trees).
* **Why**: R² = 0.987 and a tight, near-linear relationship between FIA's HTCD 4 height and (earlier measured height x growth) is exactly what you would expect if FIA's own procedure is built from each tree's own measurement history, not just its current diameter — consistent with the one documented procedure we found for this region (Woo, Eskelson & Monleon 2020, for PNW-FIA: unmeasured heights are obtained by adding the modeled height *increment* to the tree's earlier measured height). FIADB also has a dedicated field for this (`PREV_HT_FLD`, "previous total height... from the previous inventory (HTCD = 1, 2 or 3)"), confirming the earlier height is available to whatever computed HTCD 4. **This makes the anchor test not fully independent for FIA's number** — if its procedure uses the same earlier height, of course it tracks a growth projection from that height closely. The result is better read as an internal-consistency check that explains *why* FIA's number is close, not a blind horse race. For our regional model, which never sees growth history, the test is a fair, independent look at a real weakness: a diameter-only cross-sectional model cannot represent that an individual tree has already grown past what its diameter alone predicts, and the gap grows with tree size.

## Practical takeaway
A DBH-only model of the kind used for the main study's replacement of *all* non-measured heights (which does include HTCD 4 trees) is measurably worse than FIA's own procedure at tracking individual-tree growth in this region, most likely because it discards information (the tree's own remeasurement history) that FIA's procedure appears to use. This does not overturn the main study's population-level conclusions (aggregate volume/biomass change is a design-based total across millions of trees, most of them not remeasured pairs at all), but it is a concrete mechanism for the size-dependent bias documented there, and a reason to prefer a growth-based fallback over a purely cross-sectional one specifically for remeasured trees, where the data to do so already exists in FIADB.
